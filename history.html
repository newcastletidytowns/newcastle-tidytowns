Our story

A short history of Newcastle Tidy Towns

How a few volunteers in a small rural village grew into the group that looks after Newcastle today.

The early years

Newcastle Lyons Tidy Towns was started by a local resident, Eamonn Kerrigan, in the early 2000s. He was soon joined by a small number of volunteers who did regular litter picks and planted some beds. Of course, Newcastle was just a small rural village at that time. Eamonn held regular meetings and started entering the Tidy Towns competition.

Carrying it on

When Eamonn decided to retire, Breda Flatley, Gay Caffrey and Declan Sheerin made a decision to continue on with Tidy Towns rather than let it die. They started planting new beds and getting planters made to place around the village. They got stone surrounds on some beds and ordered Welcome signs for Newcastle. The litter pick is done on a regular basis.

A growing group of volunteers

We started looking for extra volunteers, and luckily we now have a great group of committed people who work on Wednesday and Saturday mornings. Of course, the cart is our pride and joy and is greatly admired.

The competition

Every year we enter the SuperValu Tidy Towns competition, and on average we gain between 12 and 15 points every year. Declan is the leader at the moment, but he is ably assisted by Breda and the team of volunteers. As the village grows, so also does the workload. We are now in the small town category according to the Tidy Towns, which is based on the population.

Support along the way

We are grateful to South Dublin County Council, who give us generous grants every year that cover the cost of plants and flowers. Storage for tools became an issue, and we were fortunate to be given the use of a shed by Rosemary Murphy on the Main Street. Last year we bought a steel shed, which is in the church car park, and we are grateful to Fr Ryan, who gave us permission.

A changing focus

Tidy Towns have now changed their emphasis from planting and litter pick to Nature and Biodiversity, Sustainability, Climate Change and pollinator-friendly planting. We also decided to have some utility boxes around the village painted with colourful murals to cut down on graffiti.

Giving back

Tidy Towns is very rewarding, and of course it is nice to give back to society and also help the environment. We always welcome new people, so if you can spare an hour or two, you might consider joining us to help enhance our village even more.

Fancy lending a hand?

An hour or two on a Wednesday or Saturday morning makes a real difference. New faces are always welcome.
